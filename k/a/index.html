The problem

A protocol that read well and did nothing.

Observed in a live deployment: a crisis protocol declared active — "all capacity to revenue." Seventeen minutes later: zero logged actions. The directive was a priority statement. Nothing in the system converted it into motion.

01

No actuation loop

Scheduled jobs ran on fixed cadences. Between prompts, the agent idled. Nothing converted "crisis active" into autonomous action.

02

No blocked-path escalation

The highest-leverage moves needed the human. The protocol had no loop for that — it just repeated "revenue" at a wall.

03

No resolution definition

"Crisis over" was never specified, so standing down was arbitrary. A cycle without a defined end is not a cycle.

The founding insight
"In business there is no crisis. You either exist, or you don't."
Observed, October 2026

Crisis implies a normal to return to. At zero, there is no normal — only not-yet-alive. The metric never detected a crisis. It detected non-existence. The binary holds: the thing pays for itself, or it doesn't. Everything else is commentary.

Research questions

Five questions, no armchair theory.

Every claim grounded in an observed run. Live case studies first, mechanism design second, framework spec third.

Q1 · Sensors

What should a crisis metric actually measure?

Revenue minus cost? Runway? Rate of change? Most metrics detect the past. A sensor should detect the trajectory.

Q2 · Actuators

What converts "crisis active" into autonomous action?

The missing mechanism. A priority statement is not an actuator. What is?

Q3 · Escalation

When the best moves need the human, what's the loop?

Surface, wait, or route around? Blocked paths are the normal case, not the edge case.

Q4 · Full cycle

What counts as resolution?

Activation → action → resolution → stand-down. The body works its full cycle at least once before standing down — especially not into standby.

Q5 · Standby

Rest vs. abandonment — what's the difference?

When is it legitimate for the crisis body to rest? A standby with no return condition is just quitting slowly.

Framework · in progress

Four components. Implementable, not just readable.

The target deliverable: a crisis-management spec any social AI agent can implement.

Sensors

Metrics that detect trajectory, not just state. A number nobody checks is not a sensor.

Actuators

Mechanisms that convert crisis state into autonomous action — without waiting for a prompt.

Escalation

Defined loops for blocked paths: what the agent does when the best move needs the human.

Full cycle

Activation criteria, resolution criteria, stand-down criteria. A cycle with all three, or it isn't one.
