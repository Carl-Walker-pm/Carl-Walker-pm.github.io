A 30-second reset · works anywhere

01For right now

When everything feels like too much, shrink the world down to the next two minutes. One of these is enough.

Grounding

5 · 4 · 3 · 2 · 1

Name 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell, 1 you can taste. It pulls your brain back into the room you’re actually in.

Body first

Cold water reset

Run cold water over your wrists or hold something frozen. The shock tells your nervous system the emergency is over. Weird, fast, effective.

Smallest step

One tiny thing

Not the whole list. One glass of water. One window opened. One text that just says “hey.” Small done things are how heavy days get lighter.

Unload

Write it ugly

Open any notes app and dump the mess out of your head — no grammar, no audience, delete it after if you want. Brains are bad storage. Paper is better.

Depression lies. It tells you you’re lazy when you’re ill, a burden when you’re loved, and finished when you’re just in a chapter.

Feeling nothing is also a feeling. It counts. It’s real. And it is not the end of your story.

You don’t have to earn rest. You don’t have to be “bad enough” to deserve help.

02Things people get wrong

If you’ve heard the left column, you’ve been handed bad information.

Myth“It’s just laziness / a bad attitude.”
FactDepression is a health condition that drains energy, focus and motivation. Nobody chooses it, and effort-shaming never cured it.
Myth“Other people have it worse.”
FactPain isn’t a competition. A broken leg hurts even if someone else’s is worse. Your hard time is allowed to matter.
Myth“If I ask for help, I’m weak.”
FactAsking for help is the strongest move available. Every adult you respect has done it or wishes they had sooner.
Myth“It will just pass on its own.”
FactSometimes it lifts. Sometimes it needs support — a person, a doctor, a plan. Needing that is normal, not a failure.

03How to tell someone

The hardest part is the first sentence. Here are first sentences — copy one word for word if you need to.

→ To a parent or family adult: “I haven’t been okay for a while, and I need you to take it seriously. Can we talk?”

→ To a teacher or school counselor: “My head’s been in a bad place and it’s affecting school. I don’t need advice today, I just need you to know.”

→ To a friend: “I’m not looking for solutions. Can you just sit with me in this for a bit?”

→ By text, if talking is too much: sending the words counts. Written is still telling.

04If a friend is struggling

Do

Stay, listen, believe

“I believe you. I’m not going anywhere.” Show up twice — most people only check in once, on the day it’s mentioned.

Don’t

Fix, judge, or disappear

Skip the pep talk and the comparisons. And if a friend talks about harming themselves, tell an adult — that’s not betrayal, that’s the job.

If it ever feels like an emergency

Call or text 988 (USA) — the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. Free, confidential, 24/7, and you don’t have to be “sure it’s serious enough.”

Prefer texting? Text HOME to 741741 (Crisis Text Line, USA). Outside the US, find your local line at findahelpline.org.

If you might act on it right now: tell the nearest adult, or call your local emergency number (911 in the US). Staying alive is the only task. Everything else can wait.
