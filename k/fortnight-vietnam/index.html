01The Arrival

Fortnight began in Spokane, Washington — one matchmaker, one client at a time, and a single promise: a first hand-picked introduction within fourteen days. It was built quietly, on purpose. No app. No feed. No audience.

Now the club crosses the Pacific. Fortnight Vietnam is the same service, carried to a country that has always understood something the apps forgot: that the most important meetings in a life are arranged by people, not by machines.

This page is the whole of it — what the club is, how it works, what it honestly cannot do, and how to ask for a consultation. Read it the way it was written: slowly.

02Why Vietnam

Vietnam is a country of introductions. Families introduce. Friends introduce. A colleague who has known you for ten years says, I know someone — and that sentence, here, still carries weight. Fortnight simply gives that sentence a professional form.

It is also a country in motion: millions of people building careers in one city while their hearts were formed in another, returning home for Tết with everything except the one thing success was supposed to bring. The apps are here too, of course — and they work exactly as well here as everywhere else, which is to say, barely.

There is room, in a country of a hundred million people, for one small, careful, human alternative. That is the room Fortnight occupies.

03Da Nang, the Home Port

Every club needs a home port, and Fortnight's is Da Nang — a centrally-governed city on the central coast, where the Hàn River meets the sea. It is neither the capital's gravity nor the south's velocity. It is a city that still has time in it.

From Da Nang, the whole country is within reach: an hour's flight to Hanoi, an hour to Ho Chi Minh City, and the entire central coast — Huế, Hội An, Quy Nhơn — along one railway line. A matchmaker based here can work the whole country without ever becoming a stranger to it.

If you write to Fortnight Vietnam, your letter lands in Da Nang first. It is read there, by a person, with the sea in the window.

04Two Other Poles — Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City

Vietnam has two great poles, and the club honours both. Hanoi — the capital, a thousand years deep, where introductions still travel through families, old schools, and long memories. Discretion is not a feature there; it is the culture.

Ho Chi Minh City — the engine of the south, fourteen million people at full speed, where fortunes are made by thirty and the calendar never empties. There, the scarcest thing is not opportunity. It is an evening.

Fortnight keeps a chapter in each pole and its home port between them. Wherever you live in Vietnam, the consultation can begin — and the search is never limited to your own city's streets.

05What Fortnight Is

Fortnight is a private, human-led matchmaking club. You apply. If you are accepted, one matchmaker takes your case personally: a long consultation, a careful search conducted through real relationships, and introductions made one at a time, only when someone truly fits what you described.

There is no catalogue to browse and no profile of you for anyone to find. You are not inventory. The people you are introduced to are not inventory either — each introduction is arranged with both sides' informed consent, at a pace both sides set.

Think of it as the oldest technology in the world — a trusted person who knows people — run with modern discipline: written confidentiality, honest feedback, and a clock.

06What Fortnight Is Not

Honesty is the club's first rule, so let this be plain. Fortnight is not a dating app: there is nothing to download, nothing to swipe, and no one will ever see your photograph on a screen you didn't approve.

It is not a database. There is no list of members to buy, rent, or leak; your story lives in one conversation with one matchmaker. It is not a party service — no mixers, no speed dating, no events where loneliness is dressed up as networking.

And it is not a guarantee of love. What it guarantees is narrower and, we think, more honourable: real introductions, to real, considered people, within fourteen days — and the truth after every one of them.

08Step One — The Consultation

Everything begins with a conversation — private, unhurried, and one to one. In person where geography allows, by video where it doesn't. Who you are. What your life actually looks like. What you are looking for, and — just as important — what you have finished compromising on.

Nothing is written down without your word. There is no intake form that reduces you to filters; the form on this page only opens the door. The consultation is where the real portrait is drawn, in your own language wherever possible, and at your pace.

Some consultations end with a polite no — from either side. That is not a failure of the process. It is the process working.

09Step Two — The Curation

After the consultation, the matchmaker goes to work the way matchmakers always have: through people. Colleagues, old friends, quiet networks built over years in Vietnam's cities — and, where it helps, introductions arranged through the club's other chapters.

Every candidate is considered against what you actually said, not against a type. Character before photographs. Circumstances before chemistry. The question is never who is impressive — it is who fits this specific life, at this specific moment.

You will not see a shortlist. When the right person is found, you will hear one name, with the reasons. One at a time, never a catalogue.

10Step Three — The Introduction

An introduction at Fortnight is a small, deliberate ceremony. Both people have agreed. Both know a little — enough to be curious, not enough to have decided. The setting is chosen with care: a quiet café in Da Nang, a gallery hour in Hanoi, an early dinner in Saigon where the evening can end gracefully at any point.

No one's surname, employer, or contact details travel ahead of them. You meet as two people, not two dossiers. What happens next is entirely yours — the club arranges the door, and then steps back from it.

Within fourteen days of your consultation, this moment happens. That is the promise, kept in daylight.

11Step Four — The Feedback

After every introduction, we talk. What worked. What didn't. What surprised you — the detail you didn't know you cared about until it was sitting across the table. This conversation is not a courtesy; it is the engine of the whole service.

Your matchmaker is learning you. Each introduction is sharper than the last because the portrait gets truer: the second search knows what the first one taught. Members often say the feedback conversation alone was worth the engagement — an honest mirror is a rare thing to be offered.

And if the news is hard — if something isn't working — you will hear it directly, from a person, not from silence.

12The Human Difference

An algorithm optimises for your attention. A matchmaker optimises for your life. That single difference explains everything about how Fortnight is built: no engagement metrics, no streaks, nothing designed to keep you searching.

A human notices what a form can't: the pause before you describe your last relationship, the quality you call "not important" three times in one hour. A human can also tell you, kindly and in time, when what you're asking for and what you actually want have quietly parted company.

Technology has its place — this page is proof. But the introduction itself, the club believes, should be made the way it has been made for centuries in Vietnam: by someone who knows both names.

13Who It Is For

Fortnight Vietnam is for professionals with full lives — founders, physicians, engineers, artists, officials — whose time is the scarcest thing they own, and who would rather spend it on the date than on the search.

It is for private people: those whose position, family, or temperament makes a public dating profile impossible, and who need the search itself to be invisible. 14Confidentiality, in Writing

Discretion at Fortnight is not a promise — it is paperwork. Every engagement begins with a signed confidentiality agreement, binding the club before it binds you.

Your name appears nowhere public. No photograph of you is shared without your explicit permission, for each introduction, every time. The people you meet know only what you have agreed they may know, and the same protection covers them — which is why interesting people are willing to be in the room at all.

When an engagement ends, it ends completely: notes are returned or destroyed at your instruction. No one will ever know you were looking, unless you choose to tell the story yourself — perhaps, one day, at a wedding.

16Honest Limits

A club that promises everything deserves your suspicion, so here are the limits, in writing. Fortnight guarantees introductions — considered, consented, on time. It cannot guarantee outcomes: chemistry is not a deliverable, and anyone who sells it as one is selling something else.

The club is small by design. Capacity is limited, applications are sometimes declined, and smaller cities may mean a search that ranges wider geographically. Pricing is discussed privately in consultation — there are no subscriptions and no hidden fees, and no one will ever be charged for a search that hasn't begun.

What you are promised is effort, honesty, and discretion, delivered by a person. In our experience, that is precisely the combination the apps cannot offer — and precisely the one that works.

20A Closing Word

Vietnamese has a word, duyên — the fated affinity that brings two people into the same room at the right moment in their lives. The old belief is that duyên cannot be forced. The club's belief is more modest: duyên can be helped. A room can be chosen. A moment can be arranged. Two names can be spoken by someone who knows them both.

That is all Fortnight does, and it is enough. Fourteen days from a conversation to a door opening — in Da Nang, in Hanoi, in Saigon, and in every city between them.
