04 · Fundamental architecture

Every Actor Is a Sensor

Your words
The fundamental idea of the system is that all actors in the system have equal potential emotional sensitivity to the system. In interaction, they exchange all possible kinds and levels of emotion.
Editorial expansion

This is the most important architectural clarification so far.

The proposition is not that all actors feel the same way. Nor does it claim that everyone has identical psychological traits. The equality concerns something more fundamental: equal standing as a potential sensor of the system's emotional condition.

Plurality, not averaging

One participant may experience joy; another anxiety; another resentment; another hope. The system should not erase these differences by immediately producing a single average.

Instead, it should preserve the plurality of signals and their intensities.

Each actor is simultaneously a source of emotional information, a receiver of information, an interpreter of events, a participant in the atmosphere, and a sensor of the whole.

Emotion as exchange

Interaction carries an enormous range of signals: trust, distrust, recognition, neglect, support, tension, hope, fear, interest, indifference, expectation, gratitude, resentment—and countless gradations between them.

The system is therefore not merely a container in which emotions happen.

The system continuously perceives itself through its actors.

The important safeguard

An emotional signal is not automatically an objective fact about the external world.

“I was treated unfairly” may be an interpretation that others contest. But the existence of the experience is itself a fact about the system: a participant experienced the system as unfair.

This distinction allows the model to avoid two opposite failures: treating every interpretation as unquestionable truth, or treating subjective experience as irrelevant.

That balance is essential if the system is to have a genuine Sense of Justice rather than merely an ideology of agreement.
