The Team Is Not the Work

Your words
It is not about work. It is about the Sense of Justice and the “Climate in the Team.”
Editorial expansion

This clarification changes the center of gravity of the idea. The subject is not productivity, performance, or the division of labor. It is the human atmosphere of the group.

A team has a climate: an emergent condition produced by how its members experience one another. Trust, recognition, belonging, tension, resentment, warmth, suspicion, gratitude, and hope are not merely private states. Through interaction, they become properties of the shared environment.

The Sense of Justice as a system signal

Suppose one member says, “This is unfair.” The system does not have to declare that person's interpretation objectively correct. But it should not dismiss the statement either.

Something undeniable has occurred: a participant is experiencing the system as unjust.

The distinction is fundamental. The interpretation may be disputed; the experience itself is part of the system's state.

Justice is not sameness

Justice does not require every member to feel equally happy, equally satisfied, or equally comfortable. It requires that each member's experience can enter the common space without being automatically ranked below someone else's.

Justice is not the demand that everyone feel the same. It is the recognition that everyone's experience matters.

A healthy climate therefore is not a state without disagreement. It is a state in which disagreement can be expressed, heard, examined, and incorporated into the system's understanding of itself.

The central question changes from “Who is right?” to “What is happening between us, and what does the whole need in order to flourish?”
