AI and the General Assembly

Your words
AI can simulate or provide its own subjective view of this concept. It is considered at a general assembly.
Editorial expansion

AI occupies an unusual place in this architecture. Its role is not necessarily to claim human emotion. It can instead provide an additional perspective on the emotional information already present in the system.

It may identify patterns that individual participants cannot see, compare competing interpretations, surface asymmetries, model possible consequences, or articulate a perspective that the group has not yet formulated.

A perspective, not a verdict

The phrase “subjective view” needs care. An AI perspective may be simulated, constructed, or model-dependent rather than humanly felt. That does not make it useless. It means its status must remain explicit.

The AI contribution is therefore another voice in the deliberative field—not a superior authority.

AI may help the system see itself. It should not become the system's judge.

The General Assembly

The assembly becomes the place where the plurality of experiences and interpretations is brought together.

Individual signals are gathered. AI perspectives are introduced. Contradictions are made visible. Participants can challenge interpretations and add context. The group then considers what the combined picture says about the state of the system.

The central question is not merely:

“Who is right?”

It is:

“What is the state of our shared system, and what should we change?”

The feedback loop

Actors → interaction → emotional signals → interpretation → AI perspectives → General Assembly → adjustment → new state.

Then the cycle begins again.

The purpose is not endless discussion of feelings. It is to give the system a way to perceive itself, deliberate about what it perceives, and deliberately move toward Flourishing.

In a mature system, this expanded perception can make the General Assembly less reactive: differences can be surfaced before they harden into conflict.
