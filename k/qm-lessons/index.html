1What the lessons are

Quality management is a trade, and it is taught here the way a trade is learned: by doing the work on real material — yours. Each set of lessons pairs a small body of reading with an assignment carried out on your own work, in writing. Competence through practice, not through slogans.

The first set — the map

  • Quality management — what the discipline is, and what it is for.
  • PDCA — the Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle: the working loop underneath everything else.
  • Kaizen — improvement as a daily habit, in small steps that hold.
  • ISO 9000 — the foundations: how quality is specified, documented, and kept.

The second set — the teacher and the tools

  • W. Edwards Deming — the teacher who rebuilt an industry's idea of quality.
  • Total quality management — quality as the whole operation's habit, not one department's job.
  • Root cause analysis — how to stop fixing symptoms.
  • Five whys — the smallest tool in the trade: ask why five times, and the real cause usually stands up.

Later sets move to Six Sigma ideas, Pareto charts and Ishikawa diagrams, and to the difference between quality control and quality assurance — and turn the tools onto one real process of yours, ending in a one-page checklist you can run without me.

2How a lesson works

A lesson is one hour, one to one. We take the current set's reading, and then we work — on your work. The structure is applied to a real process, a real defect, a real week of yours; nothing is rehearsed on invented examples.

Between sessions there is homework, in writing. After the first set: explain the PDCA cycle back in your own words. After the second: take any defect from your own week — a missed message, a lost sale, a late start — run the five whys on it, and bring the chain. The next lesson begins from what you wrote.

Guidance in music and practice is part of the same track: the lessons treat your practice — musical or otherwise — as work that can be structured, measured honestly, and improved on purpose.

3Who it is for

People who run their own work: an independent practice, a small operation, a body of creative work — and who feel the cost of doing it without structure. If your week contains missed messages, lost sales, and late starts, and you would rather find their causes than keep paying for their symptoms, these lessons are built for you.

No prior management training is assumed. What is assumed is real work to bring, and the willingness to do the written assignments between sessions.
