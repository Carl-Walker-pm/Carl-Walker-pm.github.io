01Why handmade

Material

Only natural yarn

Wool, merino, alpaca, cotton. No squeaky synthetics: natural fibers warm, breathe and do not build up static.

One of a kind

There is no second piece like it

Each piece is knitted in a single copy, for one particular person. The small particularities of handwork are not flaws — they are the maker's signature.

Lifespan

Years, not a season

A factory sweater stretches out over one winter. A hand-knitted one, with ordinary care, serves 5–10 years and outlives fashion more than once.

Ecology

Slow fashion

A made-to-order piece does not gather dust in a warehouse or end up in landfill a month later. Leftover yarn goes into children's pieces and swatches — nothing is wasted.

12How to care for wool

Seldom, gently, in warm water — and wool serves for years.

  • Wash seldom and gently: by hand or in the machine on the “wool” setting, in cool water, with a special detergent.
  • Do not wring: gently press it in a towel.
  • Dry lying flat on a towel, giving the piece its shape — away from the radiator.
  • Store folded, not on a hanger; in summer — with lavender or cedar against moths.
  • Air it out: wool loves fresh air and is often “washed” simply by a walk out onto the balcony.
  • Pilling is removed with a special fabric shaver or a soft brush — not by hand.
  • Stains are best blotted right away, not rubbed: wool does not like friction while wet.

25Knitting and health

The hands work. The head rests. The breathing itself grows more even.

A little about the craft itself — why knitting has not gone out of fashion for hundreds of years:

  • Calm: the rhythmic movements of the stitches work like a meditation and help you switch off after a hard day;
  • Supple fingers: fine motor work keeps the joints of the hands mobile;
  • A clear head: counting stitches and reading patterns is exercise for attention and memory;
  • The visible result: in a world of screens, a thing you can touch and give as a gift brings a special sense of completion.

That is why every piece from the shop also carries the maker's good mood within it. It has been checked: in a bad mood, the knitting simply will not go.

42Rescuing pieces after washing

Warm water, a towel, and patience. Millimeter by millimeter — back into shape.

Do not hurry to say goodbye. Knitted things know how to forgive mistakes — if they are approached calmly.

The piece has shrunk

It happens: you lift from the water not a sweater, but its child-sized copy. Sometimes it can be brought back — warm water, a gentle agent, patience, and slow stretching into shape, millimeter by millimeter. Send a photograph and the yarn's composition: we will say honestly, without promises, whether there is hope. Sometimes — there is.

The piece has stretched

This is rescued more often. The right washing and drying on a towel, in quiet and away from the radiator, bring back the shape better than any words. And ribbing that has tired of holding will simply be reknitted — and the piece will fit again as on the first day.

53How to store knits in summer

Summer is a time of sleep for woolen things. Nearby — lavender.

Summer is a time of sleep for woolen things. Put them away properly, and they will wake in autumn as good as new.

  • Before storing, it is best to wash an item or at least air it out: moths choose exactly what has been worn.
  • Fold — do not hang: hangers stretch knitted fabric within a single season.
  • Nearby — lavender, cedar, or a piece of soap in a cotton pouch: quiet, smelling of home, and keeping moths away.
